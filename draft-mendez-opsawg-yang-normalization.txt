   Normalized Representation of YANG Data for Equivalence Evaluation
             draft-mendez-opsawg-yang-normalization-latest

Abstract

   This document defines a normalization procedure for YANG-modeled
   information.The procedure enables different serialized
   representations conveying equivalent information to produce the same
   normalized representation independently of the input encoding and
   applicable representation conventions.. The normalized representation
   is intended to support comparison, hashing, integrity verification,
   provenance, and other procedures that require consistent
   representation of equivalent YANG-modeled information.

1.  Introduction

   YANG is widely used to model configuration data, operational state,
   notifications, and other information exchanged between network
   management systems.

   Data modeled using YANG can be represented through different
   encodings, including XML, JSON, and CBOR.  Furthermore, equivalent
   YANG-modeled information may be serialized differently by distinct
   implementations due to representation-specific conventions, ordering
   choices, or encoding mechanisms.

   These differences make it difficult to determine whether two
   representations convey the same information.  As a result, operations
   such as datastore comparison, data deduplication, fingerprint
   generation, integrity verification, provenance processing, and
   message-broker compaction often depend on representation-specific
   artifacts rather than on the underlying information itself.

   Existing canonicalization mechanisms operate at the encoding level.
   For example, XML, JSON, and CBOR provide independent procedures for
   producing deterministic encodings of individual documents.  However,
   these mechanisms do not address the broader problem of obtaining a
   common representation across different encodings.

   This document defines a normalization procedure for YANG-modeled
   information that produces a deterministic representation independent
   of the original encoding.  The procedure enables equivalent YANG-
   modeled information to converge into the same normalized
   representation, providing a stable basis for comparison, fingerprint
   generation, integrity verification, and other operational uses.

   This initial version focuses on the fundamental normalization
   procedure.  The YANG schema information, if any, required to
   construct the normalized representation remains to be determined.

1.1.  Scope

   This document focuses on representation equivalence of YANG-modeled
   data.

   The procedure defined in this document is intended to identify when
   different serialized representations convey equivalent information.

   This initial version focuses on the fundamental normalization
   procedure.  The treatment of schema-specific semantics, including
   default values, identityref and leafref resolution, and ordered-by-
   user semantics, is not yet fully specified.

   Further versions of this document are expected to determine whether
   these aspects require specific normalization rules or are inherently
   addressed by the general normalization procedure.  Where specific
   handling is required to preserve equivalence, the corresponding rules
   will be incorporated into the normalization procedure.

2.  Conventions and Definitions

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

   *  Representation: A concrete serialized encoding of YANG-modeled
      data.  Examples include XML, JSON, and CBOR encodings conveying
      the same underlying information.

   *  Normalized Representation: A deterministic representation
      generated by the normalization procedure defined in this document.

   *  Representation Equivalence: Two representations are
      representation-equivalent when they produce identical normalized
      representations.

   *  Fingerprint: A cryptographic digest calculated from a normalized
      representation.

   *  Schema-Independent Normalization: A normalization procedure that
      does not require access to the corresponding YANG schema during
      processing.

   *  Semantic Equivalence: The property whereby two representations
      correspond to the same YANG-modeled information after considering
      all schema-dependent semantics.

   Semantic equivalence is outside the scope of this version of the
   document.

3.  Problem Statement

   YANG-modeled data can be represented using different encodings,
   including XML [RFC7950], JSON [RFC7951], and CBOR [RFC9254].

   Existing canonicalization mechanisms operate within a particular
   encoding and produce deterministic representations of individual
   documents.  Examples include XML Canonicalization (XML-C14N), the
   JSON Canonicalization Scheme (JCS) [RFC8785], and the deterministic
   encoding rules defined for CBOR [RFC8949].

   These mechanisms enable a representation expressed using a particular
   encoding to be transformed into a deterministic byte sequence.
   However, they do not provide a common representation across different
   encodings, nor do they necessarily address all representation
   variations that may arise from encoding rules or implementation
   choices.

   Ordering is one possible source of such variation.  These differences
   may arise from implementation choices, internal datastore
   representations, module augmentations, or serialization procedures.

   For example, one implementation may produce:

   <aaa>
     <bbb/>
     <ccc/>
     <ddd/>
   </aaa>

   while another implementation may produce:

   <aaa>
     <ddd/>
     <bbb/>
     <ccc/>
   </aaa>

   If the order of these nodes is not semantically significant, both
   serialized representations convey equivalent information despite
   having different element ordering and byte sequences.

   As a result, equivalent YANG-modeled information may produce
   different serialized representations and therefore different
   fingerprints, hashes, or signatures.  Direct comparison of serialized
   representations cannot reliably determine whether two representations
   convey the same information.

   A normalization procedure operating above the encoding-specific
   representation layer is therefore needed to enable representation-
   independent comparison of YANG-modeled data.

3.1.  Operational Use Cases

   The absence of a representation-independent form of YANG-modeled data
   affects several operational scenarios, including:

   *  *Datastore State Comparison:* Network management systems
      frequently compare datastore snapshots obtained from different
      devices, controllers, or processing stages.  Equivalent YANG-
      modeled data represented through different encoding formats or
      ordering conventions may appear different when compared directly,
      despite conveying the same information.

   *  *Message Broker Applications:* YANG-modeled information is
      increasingly exchanged through message broker infrastructures and
      data distribution systems.  Consistent representation of
      equivalent information can support operations such as message
      deduplication, topic compaction, change detection, and content
      synchronization.

   *  *Data Synchronization and Replication:* Operational environments
      frequently exchange YANG-modeled data between multiple systems.  A
      deterministic normalized representation can simplify data
      comparison, synchronization procedures, and consistency
      verification across distributed environments.

   *  *Data Deduplication:* Storage and processing systems may benefit
      from the ability to identify equivalent information independently
      of the original representation.  A normalized representation
      provides a stable basis for content identification and
      deduplication.

   *  *Provenance and Integrity Verification:* Where stable and
      deterministic representations are required to generate
      reproducible fingerprints, hashes, and signatures and provenance
      records.

4.  Normalization Procedure

   The normalization procedure takes a serialized YANG data document as
   input and produces a deterministic normalized representation.

4.1.  Abstract Data Tree

   The procedure parses the input document according to its encoding and
   constructs an abstract data tree.  This tree provides a common,
   encoding-independent representation of the data-node structure
   exposed by the serialized document.  The abstract data tree
   represents YANG data instances.  It is not an abstract syntax tree of
   a YANG module and is not constructed by parsing YANG schema
   statements.

   The tree contains a synthetic document root.  The synthetic root
   provides a common container for the top-level nodes of the serialized
   input document.  It does not correspond to a YANG data node and uses
   an empty identifier reserved for this purpose.

   Each node below the synthetic document root has a non-empty
   identifier and is one of the following:

   *  a leaf node, which contains a textual value and has no child
      nodes; or

   *  an inner node, which contains zero or more child nodes and has no
      textual value.

   A node cannot contain both a textual value and child nodes.  The
   abstract data tree preserves node multiplicity.  Multiple nodes may
   have the same identifier, and identical nodes are not implicitly
   removed.

4.2.  Tree Construction

   The input document is first parsed according to the syntax and
   structure of the selected encoding.  Malformed input or an
   unsupported document structure causes normalization to fail.

   The procedure creates the synthetic document root and adds the top-
   level data nodes obtained from the serialized document as its
   children.

   The following conceptual rules are then applied:

   1.  An XML element, JSON object member, or CBOR map entry that
       contains nested data is represented as an inner node.

   2.  An XML element, JSON object member, or CBOR map entry that
       contains a scalar value is represented as a leaf node.

   3.  The name exposed by the input encoding is used to derive the node
       identifier.

   4.  A sequence of data items is represented as sibling nodes having
       the identifier associated with the sequence.  The sequence itself
       does not produce an additional node.

   5.  An empty sequence produces no nodes.

   6.  An empty structured value is represented as an inner node having
       no children.

   7.  The encoding-specific representation of the YANG empty type is
       represented as a leaf having an empty textual value.

   8.  Scalar values are converted to textual form.

   9.  The same construction rules are applied to every nested data item
       until the complete abstract data tree has been constructed.

   These rules are recursive in definition.  An implementation is not
   required to use recursion internally, provided that it produces the
   same abstract data tree.

   XML attributes are not represented in the abstract data tree defined
   by this version.  XML elements and their textual content are
   processed as data-tree nodes and values, respectively.

   *TBD:* Specify the treatment of YANG metadata annotations, namespace-
   qualified identifiers, namespace declarations, format-specific
   constructs, and malformed or unsupported input structures.

   The same YANG-modeled information SHOULD produce the same normalized
   representation regardless of the original serialization format.

                XML / JSON / CBOR
                         |
                         v
             Encoding-Specific Parser
                         |
                         v
               Abstract Data Tree
                         |
                         v
          Identifier and Value Processing
                         |
                         v
        Deterministic Subtree Encoding

               Child Node Sorting

5.  Examples

   This section illustrates how equivalent YANG-modeled data represented
   using different serialization formats and encodings can produce the
   same normalized representation.  The following XML and JSON
   representations examples are based on the interface data model
   originally defined in [RFC7223] and convey equivalent information.

   <interfaces xmlns="urn:ietf:params:xml:ns:yang:ietf-interfaces"
       xmlns:iana-if-type="urn:ietf:params:xml:ns:yang:iana-if-type"
       xmlns:vlan="http://example.com/vlan">
     <interface>
       <name>eth0</name>
       <type>iana-if-type:ethernetCsmacd</type>
       <enabled>false</enabled>
     </interface>
     <interface>
       <name>eth1</name>
       <type>iana-if-type:ethernetCsmacd</type>
       <enabled>true</enabled>
       <vlan:vlan-tagging>true</vlan:vlan-tagging>
     </interface>
     <interface>
       <name>eth1.10</name>
       <type>iana-if-type:l2vlan</type>
       <enabled>true</enabled>
       <vlan:base-interface>eth1</vlan:base-interface>
       <vlan:vlan-id>10</vlan:vlan-id>
     </interface>
     <interface>
       <name>lo1</name>
       <type>iana-if-type:softwareLoopback</type>
       <enabled>true</enabled>
     </interface>
   </interfaces>

   {
     "ietf-interfaces:interfaces": {
       "interface": [
         {
           "enabled": true,
           "name": "lo1",
           "type": "iana-if-type:softwareLoopback"
         },
         {
           "vlan:vlan-id": 10,
           "enabled": true,
           "type": "iana-if-type:l2vlan",
           "vlan:base-interface": "eth1",
           "name": "eth1.10"
         },
         {
           "enabled": false,
           "type": "iana-if-type:ethernetCsmacd",
           "name": "eth0"
         },
         {
           "vlan:vlan-tagging": true,
           "name": "eth1",
           "enabled": true,
           "type": "iana-if-type:ethernetCsmacd"
         }
       ]
     }
   }

   The following non-normative textual tree illustrates the intended
   outcome: equivalent XML, JSON, and CBOR representations are expected
   to produce the same normalized representation.

   interfaces
   ├─ interface
   │  ├─ enabled = true
   │  ├─ name = lo1
   │  └─ type = iana-if-type:softwareLoopback
   ├─ interface
   │  ├─ enabled = false
   │  ├─ name = eth0
   │  └─ type = iana-if-type:ethernetCsmacd
   ├─ interface
   │  ├─ enabled = true
   │  ├─ name = eth1
   │  ├─ type = iana-if-type:ethernetCsmacd
   │  └─ vlan-tagging = true
   └─ interface
      ├─ base-interface = eth1
      ├─ enabled = true
      ├─ name = eth1.10
      ├─ type = iana-if-type:l2vlan
      └─ vlan-id = 10

6.  Relationship to Existing Work

   The normalization procedure defined in this document is intended to
   complement existing efforts related to the processing,
   identification, and distribution of YANG-modeled information.

   The YANG provenance mechanisms defined in
   [I-D.ietf-opsawg-yang-provenance] operate on serialized
   representations of YANG data and can benefit from a stable,
   representation-independent normalization procedure capable of
   producing equivalent representations across different serialization
   formats.

   Similarly, work related to message broker identification and key
   generation for YANG-modeled information, such as
   [I-D.ietf-nmop-yang-message-broker-message-key], may benefit from a
   normalization procedure that produces stable representations for
   equivalent information independently of their original encoding.

   This document does not modify or replace such mechanisms.  Instead,
   it provides a potential foundation that can be used by higher-level
   procedures requiring consistent representation of equivalent YANG-
   modeled information.

7.  Security Considerations

   The normalization procedure defined in this document is intended to
   provide a deterministic representation of YANG-modeled data.  It does
   not, by itself, provide integrity, authenticity, confidentiality, or
   provenance guarantees.

   Security properties may be obtained by applying appropriate
   mechanisms, such as cryptographic hash functions, digital signatures,
   or provenance procedures, to the resulting normalized representation.
   Implementations relying on the normalized representation for
   integrity verification or provenance applications MUST use
   cryptographically secure algorithms appropriate for the intended use
   case.  Incorrect normalization procedures may result in different
   representations producing the same normalized output or in equivalent
   information producing different normalized representations.  Such
   situations may negatively affect comparison, fingerprint generation,
   and verification procedures.
